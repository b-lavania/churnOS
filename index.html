What if

What if your weekly agent review looked like this?

SaaS playbooks track seats and clicks. Agentic products fail on verified outcomes, cost per successful outcome, and trust after multi-step runs, not another trace explorer or success-rate dashboard.

What if
Paying looked healthy, but nobody got a first win?

Revenue metrics stay green while accounts never hit a verified agent outcome. The “AI churn wave” is often activation failure (time-to-first-value and paying-but-dormant accounts), not classic feature adoption.

What if
One intent burned a dozen metered steps?

Agents plan, call tools, retry, and verify; a single query becomes a cost explosion. CPSO and retry amplification show when “success” is margin-negative, especially for power users under flat pricing.

What if
The agent “worked” until humans took over?

Rising human-in-the-loop, dismissals, and scary side effects erase trust faster than usage dashboards admit. Progressive review and audit matter more than DAU.

The problem

You ship agent capabilities from traces and run logs, but traces don't tell you which ones to kill. Success rates hide retention harm until seats churn.

Long reasoning loops turn a "successful" run into uneconomic margin. Humans start dismissing agent proposals or flagging scary outputs: approval fatigue you can't quantify.

One fragile connector takes down dependent jobs, and you don't know the blast radius until support tickets spike.

And when you throttle a capability, nothing writes back to retention. You never learn if the call helped.

The solution

churnOS gives you a weekly decision list: configure your product shape, generate demo data, and review a ranked home screen: accounts at risk and capabilities to act on, each with a recommended action and estimated dollar impact if you leave them live.

Override recommendations when you disagree. Write back retention impact to close the loop, even in demo mode, you see how decisions connect to churn.

See the interactive-style preview above: accounts and capabilities ranked side by side.

Decision lenses

What the ranking is built on

The home screen ranks accounts and capabilities by estimated impact. These are the four lenses behind every recommendation: habit, trust, economics, and connector risk.

Activation
First trusted run & habit

Did seats get a successful run they trust, and keep using it weekly? Capabilities that never activate or lose delegation habit surface here first.

Trust
Approvals & trust incidents

Are humans dismissing proposals or flagging scary runs? High dismiss rates and trust incidents mean the agent is losing the room, even when runs technically succeed.

Economics
Cost per run vs seat value

Is a "successful" capability still uneconomic after loop costs? Compare what each run costs against what a seat is worth (subscription or usage-based).

Connectors
Blast radius

If one integration fails (Gmail, HubSpot, an MCP tool). How many capabilities and seats break? Fragile connectors rank higher because the fallout is wider.

How it works

Three steps to your first decision

01
Profile → Generate

Open Product Profile, pick a preset, and generate your workspace. One click fills the synthetic warehouse that powers every decision screen.

02
Radar → Review

Switch between account and capability tabs. Each card shows dollar impact, evidence chips, and a recommended action; override any card when you disagree.

03
Override → Flywheel

Disagree with a recommendation? Override and leave a reason. Outcome Flywheel writes retention impact back so you can compare followed vs overridden calls.
